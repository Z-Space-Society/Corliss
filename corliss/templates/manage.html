Two tables from two completely different places, and the difference is the point: - Applications are read live from the registry's *index*, and are written by the applicants themselves. They carry no authority whatsoever. Only the ones still awaiting a decision are listed: an application record is permanent and reads the same however it was answered, so the member cache is what tells the answered ones apart — see `views._applications` for why that question is settled by time rather than by the mere existence of a cache row. - Members are Corliss's own cache — what this deployment believes. It can be stale, incomplete, or (in the orphan case) wrong. Reconciliation is what makes it agree with the registry. **Only current members are listed.** A revoked person is history, and the registry is where history lives; readmitting them is the invite field, which writes a fresh grant. That is what readmission always was, so nothing is lost by not keeping a row for it. There is no separate admins table. Admin is a column on the member table, read from the local `is_staff` mirror, because admins are members — enforced at appointment. The service account holds no grant and so never appears here, which is deliberate: it is infrastructure, not a person, and listing it would invite somebody to try to remove it. **A DID is never text.** Handles are what a reader sees; the DID lives on the cell's `title` for the rare moment somebody needs it. `membership.handles_for` resolves them, and a grant now records the handle when it is written, so a member is named rather than numbered from the moment they are admitted. The lock in the Members heading reports whether the service account has been authenticated — the session roster writes spend, and nothing else does. Its word is **Authenticate**, never "sign in": the reader stays signed in as themselves through the whole round trip, and the wrong word makes it sound otherwise. {% endcomment %} {% block title %}Membership — ZAI{% endblock %} {% block content %}
