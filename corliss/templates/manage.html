Two tables from two completely different places, and the difference is the point: - Members are Corliss's own cache — what this deployment believes. It can be stale, incomplete, or (in the orphan case) wrong. Reconciliation is what makes it agree with the registry. - Applications are read live from the registry's *index*, and are written by the applicants themselves. They carry no authority whatsoever. Only the ones still awaiting a decision are listed: an application record is permanent and reads the same however it was answered, so the member cache is what tells the answered ones apart — see `views._applications` for why that question is settled by time rather than by the mere existence of a cache row. **Members first**, because it is the table an admin came here to read: the queue is usually empty, and a page that opens on "Nobody has applied" buries the roster under an announcement that nothing happened. **Only current members are listed.** A revoked person is history, and the registry is where history lives; readmitting them is the invite field, which writes a fresh grant. That is what readmission always was, so nothing is lost by not keeping a row for it. There is no separate admins table. Admin is a column on the member table, read from the local `is_staff` mirror, because admins are members — enforced at appointment. The service account holds no grant and so never appears here, which is deliberate: it is infrastructure, not a person, and listing it would invite somebody to try to remove it. **A DID is never text.** Handles are what a reader sees; the DID lives on the cell's `title` for the rare moment somebody needs it. `membership.handles_for` resolves them, and a grant now records the handle when it is written, so a member is named rather than numbered from the moment they are admitted. The one exception is a member's own panel, which is about exactly one person: there the DID is selectable text, because somebody who opened it to read the DID wants to copy it, and there is no column of them to keep narrow. **The member table carries no controls.** Every write — tier, revocation, admin — lives in a panel opened from the handle, because a `Change` column sets the table's width from its widest control rather than from anything anybody reads, and that is what made this table scroll sideways. The lock in the Members card reports whether the service account has been authenticated — the session roster writes spend, and nothing else does. It says **Admin Locked** / **Admin Unlocked**, naming what it governs rather than describing itself, and its verb is **Authenticate**, never "sign in": the reader stays signed in as themselves through the whole round trip, and the wrong word makes it sound otherwise. {% endcomment %} {% block title %}Membership Management — SCN{% endblock %} {% block content %}
